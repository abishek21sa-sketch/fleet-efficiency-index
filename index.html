Fleet Efficiency Index

A full-scale data-analysis & machine-learning pipeline over every EPA-tested vehicle sold in the United States — Tesla to Cadillac, Audi to Ram — spanning four decades of the fleet, cross-referenced against NHTSA safety ratings and recall history. Five regression algorithms cross-validated head-to-head, SHAP-based explainability, an unsupervised market segmentation, a powertrain classifier, and two models running live, client-side, on your spec sheet.

01 — TREND

Brand efficiency over time

Average EPA combined fuel economy per model year, by manufacturer. Pick up to six brands to compare.

Figures for fully-electric brands (Tesla, Rivian, Lucid) are EPA MPGe — an energy-equivalence scale, not directly comparable gallon-for-gallon to gasoline MPG for other brands. Shown together deliberately: the gap is the story.

02 — ELECTRIFICATION

Share of new EPA test entries that are electric

Industry-wide, all 146 makes in the dataset — the fraction of each model year's tested vehicles that run on electricity alone, with a 2027–2030 logistic-curve forecast (shaded band = 10th–90th percentile across 300 bootstrap refits).

Fit: a 3-parameter logistic curve (ceiling × growth rate × inflection point) on the historical series, refit 300 times against resampled residuals for the band. Early-stage S-curves are genuinely hard to project — the eventual ceiling is sensitive to exactly where the curve is right now, and a few more high-growth years could push the whole curve higher. Read the point forecast as "our model's best guess," not a confident industry prediction.

05 — BAKE-OFF

Five algorithms, one target

5-fold cross-validation (48,324 vehicles, mean ± std across folds — not a single lucky split), five regression algorithms predicting combined MPG. Two are simple enough to run live in your browser — the rest trade portability for accuracy.

ModelR² (mean ± std)MAE (mean ± std)

R² measures share of MPG variance explained (1.0 = perfect); mean absolute error is in MPG. 5-fold CV means each model was trained and tested 5 times on different slices of the data — the ± spread tells you how much the score would move on a different sample, which a single 80/20 split can't show. Ensemble methods (Random Forest, Gradient Boosting) win on accuracy by capturing non-linear interactions a straight line can't — the trade-off is that their decision logic can't be reduced to a formula you can run without the library.

06 — EXPLAINABILITY

What actually drives fuel economy

SHAP values from the Random Forest (mean |SHAP| across a 1,500-vehicle sample, aggregated back from one-hot categories to their parent field) — a game-theoretic attribution that doesn't share impurity-based feature importance's bias toward high-cardinality fields.

08 — CLASSIFICATION

Guessing the powertrain from the body

A Random Forest classifier predicting powertrain family — gasoline, diesel, hybrid, or fully electric — from body style, drivetrain, engine displacement and model year alone. Confusion matrix on held-out vehicles, rows normalized to the actual class.

Overall accuracy across 4 classes (25% = chance). Diagonal cells are correct calls; off-diagonal cells show where the model confuses one powertrain family for another — gasoline and hybrid share the most overlap, which tracks: a hybrid's engine displacement often looks identical to its gasoline-only sibling.
